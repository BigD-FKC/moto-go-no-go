Privacy policy

Moto Go/No-Go for iPhone and iPad · Effective October 8, 2026

HomeSupport and contact

In short: the app's maker collects nothing. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics, and no tracking. What you enter stays on your device and in your own iCloud.

What the app's maker collects

Nothing. Moto Go/No-Go has no server of its own. The app's maker, Andrew Lane, cannot see your settings, location, bikes, rides, trips, journal, fuel and maintenance records, manuals, or photos.

What stays on your device and in your iCloud

  • Your settings, bikes, rides, trips, journal entries, fuel stops, and maintenance records are stored on your device and, if you use iCloud, in your own private iCloud storage, so they reach your other devices. Apple holds that storage under its own privacy policy; the app's maker has no access to it.
  • Manuals you add stay on your device unless you choose to keep them in your iCloud.
  • Your roadside card (plate, insurance, phone numbers, registration, and insurance card) stays only on the device where you entered it. It is never put in iCloud, and it opens with Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode.
  • Photos stay in your Photos library. Photos you use to read a tire's date code or an oil label are read on your device and are not sent anywhere.
  • The app doesn't read or analyze your manuals. It shows them to you and searches them when you ask, on your device.

Your location

  • The app uses your location only when you ask: to set your home, to center the forecast where you are, to record a ride, or to note the place of a journal entry or fuel stop.
  • While a ride is recording, location keeps working with the screen locked, and the system's location indicator shows it. Your location is not followed when you aren't recording.
  • A ride you share as a GPX file leaves out the area around your home.

What the app sends to other services, and why

To do its work, the app asks public and Apple services for information. These requests carry no name, account, or advertising identifier from the app. Like any internet request, they reach the service from your device's IP address.

  • U.S. National Weather Service: the coordinates of your forecast area (your home or where you are, and the towns around it), to get the forecast, observations, and alerts.
  • NOAA Storm Prediction Center, EPA AirNow, and state transportation departments: the app downloads their public files (storm outlooks, air quality, road work) and picks out your area on your device.
  • NOAA radar: the area of the radar map you are looking at.
  • Apple Weather: a location, for the next-hour rain outlook and for the weather at a trip's stops and journal entries.
  • Apple Maps: a place you search for, or where you are, to name a town, find a gas station when you stop, or draw a map.
  • NHTSA (U.S. Department of Transportation): your bike's VIN, only when you tap Look up; and its make, model, and year, to check for safety recalls.
  • Makers' and reference websites: when you choose to find a manual or look up specs, the app opens that website for you.

Each of those services handles requests under its own privacy policy.

Beta testing with TestFlight

If you test the app through Apple's TestFlight, Apple may share crash reports and any feedback or screenshots you choose to send with the app's maker. That is handled under Apple's TestFlight terms.

Children

The app is not directed to children, and it collects no personal information from anyone.

Keeping and deleting your data

Because your data is on your device and in your own iCloud, you control it. You can delete any ride, trip, entry, record, bike, or manual in the app. Deleting the app removes its data from that device; you can remove the app's iCloud data in Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Storage.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
