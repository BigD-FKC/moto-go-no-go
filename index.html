Moto Go/No-Go

Riding weather for motorcyclists, for iPhone and iPad.

Join the betaPrivacy policySupport and contact

Moto Go/No-Go answers one question: is it a good day to ride, and where? It reads the weather for you and tells you what the riding weather is now and until when, like "GO until 4 PM, then CAUTION". Each part of the day gets a clear call, GO, CAUTION, or NO-GO, with a score out of 10 and the reason why.

It starts on a standard set of weather limits. Set your own for cold, heat, rain, lightning, wind, air quality, and riding hours, and add your bike: heated grips or a heated seat shape its rating, and wind protection its wind chill. When part of the forecast couldn't be fetched, the app says what's missing.

It rates the towns around you in eight directions, so you can see which way is best, and rates a route you've been given (a GPX file) at the time you'd reach each place.

It also records rides, keeps a trip log and a journal, and holds your bikes' specs, maintenance records, recall checks, and manuals. Widgets put the rating on your Home Screen and Lock Screen. On iPhone, ask Siri "Can I ride today in Moto Go?" and it tells you the answer in the app's own words, with how old the forecast is.

Riding outside the United States? For now the forecast works only in the US: it uses the US National Weather Service. Ride recording, the trip log, maintenance records, and the tools work anywhere. If you'd use Moto Go/No-Go where you ride, write to k12techman@gmail.com and tell us which country. It helps decide what comes next.

Tools for your bike

  • Fuel range: your economy and range from your own fuel stops, a place for the octane your manual calls for, and a plain explanation of octane.
  • Tire pressure check and Tire Demystifier: what your pressures read as the air warms or cools; what a tire's size means, how old it is, and a wear check.
  • Oil Analyzer: what your manual asks for, as you enter it, against the label on a bottle, and what a rating like 10W-40 means.
  • Battery Analyzer: the kinds of motorcycle battery, and a health check with a multimeter.
  • Unit converter, and a roadside card for your plate, insurance, and papers.

Keep your manual in the app and look things up in it as you go. You enter the figures; the tools explain them. Your manual has the final word.

Moto Go/No-Go is for general information only. It is a guide to help you decide, not a guarantee that conditions are safe. You alone decide whether and how to ride.
